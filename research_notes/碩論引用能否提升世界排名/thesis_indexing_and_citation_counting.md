# Thesis indexing and citation counting: where (if anywhere) is a citation inside a Taiwanese master's thesis counted?

Scope: whether a reference printed in a Taiwanese master's thesis (deposited in 臺灣博碩士論文知識加值系統 / NDLTD Taiwan, a university repository, or Airiti Library 華藝線上圖書館) pointing to (a) a professor's own master's thesis, (b) an earlier lab member's thesis supervised by that professor, or (c) the professor's journal or conference papers is counted as a citation in Scopus, Web of Science, OpenAlex, Google Scholar, NDLTD, TCI-HSS or ACI, and whether it accrues to the professor or to the university. Research date: 2026-10-11. Dates of older sources are flagged inline.

The session's egress proxy returned HTTP 403 for `api.openalex.org`, `ndltd.ncl.edu.tw`, `airitilibrary.com` and `scholar.google.com`, so none of those pages could be checked directly. All findings below come from search-engine retrieval of the cited pages. Where the search summary did not clearly tie a claim to one URL, the bullet says so.

## 1. Scopus and SciVal: are theses indexed, and can references inside a thesis ever produce Scopus citations, an h-index or FWCI?

### Takeaway
Scopus does not index master's theses. Its source types are ISSN serials and ISBN books and proceedings, and Elsevier's content policy says outright that dissertations are not considered as books. Scopus captures citations only from the reference lists of documents it indexes. A reference printed inside a Taiwanese master's thesis therefore never reaches Scopus. It cannot raise a Scopus "Cited by" count, a Scopus Author Profile h-index or a SciVal/Scopus FWCI, whether the cited item is the professor's thesis, a lab member's thesis or the professor's own papers.

### Cited Findings
- Scopus covers two source families: "serial publications that have an ISSN … such as journals, book series and some conference series, or non-serial publications that have an ISBN" such as one-off books or one-off conferences. — [Scopus Content Coverage Guide (current web PDF)](https://supportcontent.elsevier.com/Support%20Hub/Scopus/Scopus_ContentCoverage_Guide_WEB.pdf)
- Elsevier's Scopus content policy states that **dissertations are not considered for evaluation as books**. Book coverage is selected publisher by publisher, not title by title. The CSAB does not consider suggestions for individual books. A book needs an ISBN and a digital edition, ONIX or MARC metadata with BIC/BISAC/THEMA codes, and an English title and abstract. Covered book types are monographs, edited volumes, major reference works and graduate-level textbooks. — [Elsevier: Scopus content policy and selection](https://www.elsevier.com/products/scopus/content/content-policy-and-selection)
- A University of Michigan library guide says Scopus citation counts do not include citations from books, conference proceedings, dissertations/theses, patents, technical reports or other publication types. — [U-Michigan Research Impact guide: Scopus](https://guides.lib.umich.edu/citation/Scopus). Partly contradicted by [the Scopus Content Coverage Guide](https://supportcontent.elsevier.com/Support%20Hub/Scopus/Scopus_ContentCoverage_Guide_WEB.pdf), which lists book series, conference series and ISBN books as covered. The guide looks dated on books and proceedings, but its statement about theses agrees with Elsevier's policy.
- "Secondary documents" are Scopus's display of references to works **not indexed in Scopus** that appear in the reference lists of indexed documents. They sit on a separate "Secondary documents" tab of the results page. The entries are built from printed reference lists, may lack detail, and one work can be split across many entries (UCL's example shows one book under 125 entries). — [UCL Library guide: Scopus secondary documents](https://library-guides.ucl.ac.uk/scopus/secondary-documents); also described in Elsevier's own guides ([Scopus Quick Reference Guide 2023](https://supportcontent.elsevier.com/RightNow%20Next%20Gen/Scopus/Files/Scopus%20Quick%20Reference%20Guide%20WEB_2023.pdf); [2014 Content Coverage Guide](https://www.recursoscientificos.fecyt.es/sites/default/files/content_coverage_guide.pdf))
- Secondary documents arise mainly when Scopus is "unable to match documents with certainty due to incomplete or incorrect data". They "primarily arise from missing, incomplete, or incorrect reference lists in the citing documents". Scopus does not correct errors in reference lists, so a wrong page number can make even an indexed article appear as a secondary document. — [CityU HK Library: Understanding Secondary Documents in Scopus](https://libguides.library.cityu.edu.hk/rssc_blog/home/Understanding-Secondary-Documents-in-Scopus)
- Library guides note that secondary documents show their own citation counts, and clicking a count lists the citing (indexed) papers. — [UCL guide](https://library-guides.ucl.ac.uk/scopus/secondary-documents); [EUR guide](https://libguides.eur.nl/courseresearchimpacts/scopuscitingarticles). The search summary did not say which of these two guides made the point.
- Scopus Author Profiles are built automatically by an algorithm that matches names to **documents indexed in Scopus**, and affiliations come from document metadata. If a publication is missing from a profile, the likely reason is that it is not indexed, because journals and books must first be approved by the CSAB. Corrections go through the Author Feedback Wizard. — [Elsevier: Scopus Author Profile FAQs](https://www.elsevier.support/scopus/answer/scopus-author-profile-faqs) (the search tool reported this page as roughly two years old)
- FWCI is "the ratio of the total citations actually received by the denominator's output, and the total citations expected based on the average of the subject field". 1.00 is the world average, and "World" means the entire Scopus database. Each output is compared with outputs of the same subject field, document type and publication year. — [Elsevier Scopus support: What is FWCI?](https://www.elsevier.support/scopus/answer/what-is-fieldweighted-citation-impact); [SciVal Metric: FWCI](https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/28192/~/scival-metric%3A-field-weighted-citation-impact-%28fwci%29). A third-party paper gives the FWCI citation window as the publication year plus the three following years. — [arXiv 2408.10262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10262)
- Kousha & Thelwall (2019), summarizing earlier work as reported by the search tool, say about 1.5% of Scopus publications cited at least one dissertation (PhD 69%, master's 21%, other theses 10%). They also say thesis coverage in such databases is unknown. Theses therefore show up in Scopus only as *cited* secondary items. — [Kousha & Thelwall, arXiv 1902.08746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.08746). I could not open the PDF to check the exact wording.

### Inferences
- By definition, secondary documents are items *cited by* Scopus-indexed records. Only indexed records sit on the citing side of the Scopus citation graph, so a non-indexed thesis's reference list is never processed. Scenarios (a), (b) and (c) all add zero Scopus citations when the citing document is a master's thesis.
- When a Scopus-indexed journal or conference paper cites a master's thesis (scenarios a and b), the thesis shows up only as a secondary document with its own count. It is not part of any Author Profile, so it adds nothing to an h-index. It has no indexed affiliation, so it adds nothing to institutional output or FWCI in Scopus or SciVal, which are the data behind Scopus-based rankings.
- The only route by which the student's work reaches Scopus is a later **Scopus-indexed** paper by the student, for example in a Scopus-indexed journal or conference proceedings, that cites the professor's indexed papers. If the professor co-authors that paper, the citation is an author self-citation, which some indicators exclude. For example, the Leiden Open Edition excludes author self-citations ([Leiden Open Edition indicators](https://open.leidenranking.com/information/indicators)).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the document-type list in section 3.1 of the Content Coverage Guide verbatim (WebFetch failed), so I cannot quote whether "thesis" appears as a Scopus document type. No source said it does.
- I found no Elsevier sentence that literally says "citations from non-indexed documents are not counted". The conclusion rests on the definition of secondary documents plus the Author Profile FAQ and the FWCI definition.
- Not checked: whether thesis series published as Scopus-indexed ISSN book series (such as a "theses" book series) are indexed. This is unlikely to matter for typical Taiwanese master's theses.

## 2. Web of Science Core Collection, the ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Citation Index, and InCites

### Takeaway
The Web of Science Core Collection does not index theses. Its "Times Cited" counts only citing publications that are themselves in the Core Collection, and the Core Collection is the dataset behind InCites institutional metrics. Theses sit in the separate ProQuest Dissertations & Theses (PQDT) Citation Index, which Clarivate files under "beyond published literature". Clarivate linked PQDT's 172M+ cited references into the WoS platform in December 2023. I found no evidence that Taiwanese theses are in PQDT, and no evidence that citations from PQDT records feed Core Collection Times Cited or InCites.

### Cited Findings
- The PQDT Citation Index is a standalone database on the WoS platform, included in "All Databases" searches by default. "Dissertations and theses that have been cited by Web of Science Core Collection records will include a citation count and a link to the citing articles." — [Clarivate WoS help: ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Citation Index](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/20140311397009-ProQuest-Dissertations-Theses-Citation-Index)
- Size today: 6.6m+ dissertation and thesis records from 60+ countries and 4,000+ university contributors. — [Clarivate PQDT Citation Index product page](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/scientific-and-academic-research/research-discovery-and-referencing/web-of-science/proquest-dissertations-theses-citation-index/). At launch in 2023: 5.5M+ records from 4,100+ institutions in 60+ countries, going back to 1637. — [Clarivate news (2023)](https://clarivate.com/news/clarivate-enriches-web-of-science-platform-with-integration-of-proquest-dissertations-and-theses-global/); [Queen's University Library listing](https://library.queensu.ca/search/database/proquest-dissertations-and-theses-citation-index-web-science)
- In December 2023 Clarivate integrated "over 172 million cited references from ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global" into the WoS citation network. Each dissertation record now carries a full, browsable cited-reference list, and dissertations "will accrue citations from indexed research" over time. — [Clarivate news, Dec 2023](https://clarivate.com/news/clarivate-connects-172m-cited-references-from-proquest-dissertations-and-theses-to-the-web-of-science/); [Clarivate blog](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/discovering-new-insights-from-early-career-scholarship-through-the-addition-of-172m-linked-cited-references-from-proquest-dissertations-and-theses-on-the-web-of-science/)
- The WoS Coverage Details page lists the PQDT Citation Index under "Beyond published literature", next to the Preprint Citation Index and the Policy Citation Index. — [Clarivate WoS help: Web of Science Coverage Details](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/48137951657233-Web-of-Science-Coverage-Details)
- "Times Cited count in Web of Science Core Collection shows the number of publications that cite the current article in the Web of Science Core Collection." — [Clarivate WoS help: Core Collection Full Record Details](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/26916215746321-Core-Collection-Full-Record-Details). The All Databases citation count "will include citations from all cited reference collections", even collections the institution does not subscribe to. — [Clarivate WoS help: All Databases Search](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/20130401540497-All-Databases-Search). An All Databases citation report uses the "Sum of the Times Cited" count, which "generally differs from the Times Cited count for Web of Science Core Collection". — [Clarivate WoS help: Citation Reports](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/20016829622033-Citation-Reports). The search summary attributed the first two quotes to Clarivate help pages without naming which page, so the mapping to these two pages is my best reading.
- "The Web of Science Core Collection serves as the standard data set underpinning the journal impact metrics found in the Journal Citation Reports and the institutional performance metrics found in InCites." — quoted in a library/Clarivate guide; the search summary did not say which of these pages it came from: [NJIT WoS & InCites guide](https://researchguides.njit.edu/wos); [Clarivate LibGuide: coverage](https://clarivate.libguides.com/librarianresources/coverage)
- An older university guide says WoS does not count citations from books, dissertations/theses, patents, technical reports and similar items. — [University of Cincinnati research-impact guide: WoS](https://guides.libraries.uc.edu/c.php?g=222356&p=1474532) (the search tool flagged it as older; treat as background)
- PQDT Global is "primarily North American" with "significant and growing international coverage" through partnerships, and adds about 70,000 full-text dissertations a year from about 700 partner institutions. — [UW–Madison Libraries database entry](https://search.library.wisc.edu/database/UWI25651). The search did not say which of the two library guides it cited (this entry or the APSU guide) carried each figure.
- Taiwan and Hong Kong libraries run a **Digital Dissertation Consortium** (DDC, 國際博碩士論文全文資料庫) holding 200,000+ full-text dissertations bought from PQDT. Taiwan is a *buyer* of PQDT content here, not a contributor of Taiwanese theses. Taiwanese theses are listed separately under NDLTD Taiwan. — [CUHK Library guide: international theses](https://libguides.lib.cuhk.edu.hk/c.php?g=319005&p=2147533)

### Inferences
- A Taiwanese master's thesis is not in the Core Collection and, on the available evidence, not in PQDT. Its references are therefore captured nowhere in WoS. Core Collection Times Cited for the professor's papers (scenario c) cannot change, and InCites metrics, which are built on the Core Collection, cannot change either.
- For a thesis that *is* in PQDT (say a US dissertation), its citations to Core Collection papers would at most appear in the All Databases count, not in Core Collection Times Cited. This follows from the help-page definitions; Clarivate does not spell out the mechanics.
- Scenarios (a) and (b): a Taiwanese thesis cannot get a WoS citation count unless PQDT holds a record for it, and I found no evidence that it does.

### Gaps
- I found no official list of PQDT contributing institutions or countries, so I cannot positively confirm that Taiwanese theses are absent.
- I found no Clarivate statement confirming whether citations from PQDT records count in the All Databases Times Cited of Core Collection records (the search summary flagged this as unverified).
- I did not retrieve InCites documentation directly. The InCites-on-Core-Collection point relies on the guide quote above.

## 3. OpenAlex and the Leiden Ranking Open Edition

### Takeaway
OpenAlex has a `dissertation` work type and harvests repositories, so theses can exist there as works. Taiwanese thesis DOIs, however, are registered with the **Airiti DOI Registration Agency**, a separate agency from Crossref, and I could not check (API blocked) whether Taiwanese master's theses with parsed reference lists exist in OpenAlex. The Leiden Ranking Open Edition counts only journal publications ("core" plus, since 2025, "non-core") as a university's output, so a thesis never counts as output. Whether the Open Edition's impact indicators count citations coming *from* non-core works such as theses is not stated in any retrieved text.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAlex assigns every work exactly one type from a controlled vocabulary derived from Crossref types plus OpenAlex additions. Common values include `article`, `book`, `dataset`, `preprint`, `dissertation` and `book-chapter`. — [OpenAlex Developers: Work types](https://developers.openalex.org/api-reference/work-types); [OpenAlex help: Work types](https://help.openalex.org/data/work-types/)
- The OpenAlex works overview lists dissertations among the scholarly works it catalogs. `repository` is one source type, and institutional repositories are among OpenAlex's sources. — [OpenAlex help: Works](https://help.openalex.org/data/works/); [Source types](https://help.openalex.org/data/source-types/); [Sources](https://help.openalex.org/data/sources/)
- A new OpenAlex type classifier changed the type of 49.6 million works (about 10% of the catalog) on its first production run, so older type counts may not match current data. — [OpenAlex blog: An overhaul of type classification](https://blog.openalex.org/an-overhaul-of-type-classification/)
- OpenAlex aggregates daily from open sources including institutional and subject repositories (for example PubMed Central, arXiv and Zenodo). — [OpenAlex article, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13157964)
- The Airiti DOI RA is one of the International DOI Foundation's registration agencies, "responsible for the DOI applications of objects in Traditional Chinese", and the only IDF-authorized RA in Taiwan. — [Airiti DOI RA: About](https://doi.airiti.com/en/about_airiti_page1.html); [Airiti DOI page](https://www.airiti.com/en/page_doi.html). Airiti registers thesis DOIs at submission through its Electronic Thesis & Dissertation Service, which 21 Taiwanese universities and colleges use, NTU among them. — [Airiti ETDS page](https://www.airiti.com/en/page_etds.html). Crossref is a different agency. — [DOI Foundation: Existing registration agencies](https://www.doi.org/the-community/existing-registration-agencies/)
- Leiden Open Edition (2024 wording): "In the calculation of indicators, only core publications are taken into account." A core publication is an article or book-chapter-type item in a journal or book series, in English, not retracted, and in a core journal (international scope, with enough references to other core journals). The 2025 wording is "English-language, non-retracted journal articles in core journals". — [Leiden Open Edition: Indicators](https://open.leidenranking.com/information/indicators)
- The 2025 edition added "non-core" publications, meaning publications in venues with a national or regional focus, alongside the core set. — [Leiden Madtrics: The CWTS Leiden Ranking 2025](https://www.leidenmadtrics.nl/articles/the-cwts-leiden-ranking-2025-more-open-more-inclusive-more-informative). Earlier core criteria: one or more authors, at least one reference, English, published in a core journal. — [Leiden Madtrics: Introducing the Open Edition](https://www.leidenmadtrics.nl/articles/introducing-the-leiden-ranking-open-edition)
- In the Traditional Edition, core publications are a subset of SCIE/SSCI/A&HCI. Books, conference proceedings and journals outside those indexes are excluded, and about one-sixth of WoS publications are dropped as non-core. — [Leiden Traditional Edition: Indicators](https://traditional.leidenranking.com/information/indicators)
- The Open Edition counts citations up to the end of 2023 and excludes author self-citations. — [Leiden Open Edition: Indicators](https://open.leidenranking.com/information/indicators) (the ranking researcher covers the time windows)

### Inferences
- A master's thesis can never count as a university's publication in either Leiden edition, because both sets are journal-based.
- For a citation *from* a Taiwanese thesis to affect Leiden impact indicators, two things would have to hold. The thesis would need to be an OpenAlex work with a parsed reference list, and the Open Edition would need to count citations from all OpenAlex works rather than only from core or non-core publications. Neither is confirmed. Taiwanese thesis DOIs go through the Airiti RA, not Crossref, so OpenAlex probably holds few Taiwanese theses with references. This is unverified.
- Even in the best case, a citation inside a Taiwanese master's thesis is at most one citation to a cited journal paper inside a field-normalized indicator. I leave weighting to the ranking researcher.

### Gaps
- A direct OpenAlex API check (`type:dissertation` with `country_code:TW`, plus source lookups for NDLTD and Airiti) failed: the egress proxy returned HTTP 403 for `api.openalex.org`. Counts are unknown.
- No retrieved Leiden text says whether citing works outside the core (or non-core) set are counted in impact indicators.
- No source says whether OpenAlex ingests Airiti RA DOI metadata, or harvests NDLTD Taiwan or Taiwanese university repositories.

## 4. Google Scholar: indexing of Taiwanese theses, "Cited by" counts, profiles, and advisor mis-attribution

### Takeaway
Google Scholar is the one major global index where a citation printed inside a Taiwanese master's thesis plausibly becomes a counted citation. Scholar explicitly accepts theses from repositories. Airiti lists Google Scholar as a partner. In 2013 the National Central Library (NCL) promoted NDLTD's indexing in Google and Google Scholar. Studies find that theses are a major source of citations that only Scholar finds. Such citations accrue to the *cited record*, and through it to whichever person's Scholar profile contains that record. That is a personal metric, not the institutional bibliometric data that Scopus-, WoS- or OpenAlex-based rankings use. The one ranking-relevant exception is Webometrics, whose "Openness" indicator has historically used Google Scholar profile citations; the ranking researcher should verify this.

### Cited Findings
- Google Scholar's inclusion guidelines treat theses and dissertations as eligible scholarly content and recommend that university repositories run DSpace or Digital Commons. On authors: "The author tag must contain the authors (and only the actual authors) of the paper. Don't use it for the author of the website or for contributors other than authors, e.g., thesis advisors." — [Google Scholar: Inclusion Guidelines for Webmasters](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/inclusion.html)
- A Google Scholar presentation to repository managers says that for "uniquely held items such as dissertations" the repository copy is the primary link. Indexing needs crawler access, a list of item URLs and machine-readable meta tags such as `citation_pdf_url`. — [Google Scholar indexing for DSpace (M. Westin, Google Scholar; ~2019)](https://wiki.lyrasis.org/download/attachments/125731110/Germany_%20Google%20Scholar%20indexing%20for%20DSpace.pdf?version=1&modificationDate=1561622400395&api=v2); [CARL webinar, Jan 2021](https://www.carl-abrc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Google_Scholar_webinar_Jan2021.pdf)
- Airiti Library lists Google Scholar and NAVER Academic among its partners and covers the theses of 60+ Taiwanese colleges and universities. — [Airiti Library product page](http://www.airiti.com/tw/page_al.html)
- An NCL presentation from **2013** lists a "免費全球搜尋引擎推廣服務" (free promotion through global search engines) for NDLTD, citing Google and Google Scholar index volumes. As of 2013-05-15 NDLTD held more than 760,000 thesis abstracts and more than 255,000 authorized full texts. — [NCL, 臺灣博碩士論文知識加值系統新功能簡介 (2013-05-21)](https://lib.hfu.edu.tw/var/file/1/1001/img/55/815064612.pdf)
- Google Scholar profiles: metrics update automatically as new citations are found, and the owner cannot edit "Cited by" numbers. When versions are merged, the count is the number of distinct citing papers. An asterisked count was estimated automatically and may include citations that do not match the article. Missing citations usually mean the citing papers cannot be reached by Scholar's crawlers or have formatting the software cannot parse. Owners choose which articles appear on their profile. — [Google Scholar Profiles help](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/citations.html)
- Martín-Martín et al. (2018), *Journal of Informetrics* 12(4), studied about 2.4M citations to highly cited 2006 documents. Google Scholar found 93–96% of citations across areas, Scopus 35–77% and WoS 27–73%. "Most citations found only by GS were from non-journal sources (48%-65%), including theses, books, conference papers, and unpublished materials." — [Martín-Martín, Orduna-Malea, Thelwall & Delgado López-Cózar, arXiv 1808.05053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.05053) (2018 data)
- Kousha & Thelwall (2019) extracted Google Scholar citation counts for 77,884 US doctoral dissertations (2013–2017, via ProQuest) with over 95% precision. About a fifth had at least one Scholar citation, and dissertations draw a noticeable share of their citations from other dissertations. — [arXiv 1902.08746](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08746)
- Google Scholar "sometimes does not seem to parse the author field correctly", so records can carry wrong author lists. — [Harzing, Publish or Perish tutorial: Google Scholar wrong authors](https://harzing.com/resources/publish-or-perish/tutorial/google-scholar/wrong-authors)
- Webometrics' Openness indicator "does not take its data from a citation Index like WoS or SCOPUS, but directly from Google Scholar Citations". The 2019 notes say citations are collected from the top 100 public profiles of each university, the top five are excluded, profiles are filtered by institutional e-mail domain, and non-individual profiles are penalized. — [Webometrics research ranking, July 2019 edition notes](https://research.webometrics.info/en/current_edition); [Webometrics FAQ](https://www.webometrics.info/en/node/36). The search summary did not say which of the two pages carried each detail. A January 2025 university post says the ranking draws on Majestic, Google Scholar and Scopus, weighted Visibility 50%, Excellence 40%, Openness 10%. — [University of Malta post (2025)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/university-of-malta_ranking-web-of-universities-webometricsinfo-activity-7316058703734935553-QlnC). A newer secondary description says Openness now uses citation data linked to ROR identifiers. — [upGrad guide (2026)](https://www.upgrad.com/study-abroad/articles/webometrics-ranking-of-world-universities/). This conflicts with the 2019 notes and is unverified.

### Inferences
- **Scenario (c)**: if Google Scholar indexes the citing thesis with its reference list, either through NDLTD's open full text, Airiti's partner feed or a university repository, the professor's paper gains a Scholar "Cited by", and the professor's profile totals and h-index rise accordingly.
- **Scenario (a)**: the professor's own master's thesis must exist as a Scholar record (via NDLTD, Airiti or a repository) and be on the professor's profile, which the owner can add. Later theses that cite it then raise the professor's profile count, whatever university awarded the thesis.
- **Scenario (b)**: a lab member's thesis is the *student's* record. Its citations reach the professor's profile only if Scholar wrongly parses the advisor as an author, or if the professor adds the record by hand, which would misstate authorship. Scholar's guideline explicitly tells sites not to put thesis advisors in the author tag, which suggests advisor-as-author is a known failure mode. I found no documented Taiwanese case.
- Google Scholar profile counts feed no Scopus, WoS or OpenAlex ranking pipeline. The only ranking-relevant channel found is Webometrics Openness, whose current (2025–2026) data source conflicts across sources.

### Gaps
- I could not test whether NDLTD or Airiti thesis records appear in Google Scholar today, or whether their reference lists generate "Cited by" links: `scholar.google.com`, `ndltd.ncl.edu.tw` and `airitilibrary.com` were all blocked by the egress proxy. The NDLTD evidence dates from 2013.
- I could not inspect whether NDLTD, Airiti or Taiwanese repository pages put advisors (指導教授) into `citation_author` meta tags, so the mis-attribution risk for Taiwanese theses is unverified.
- The current Webometrics Openness methodology is unconfirmed (2019 description vs. a 2026 secondary description).

## 5. NDLTD Taiwan (臺灣博碩士論文知識加值系統): 被引用次數 / 引用關係 and any official use

### Takeaway
NDLTD shows a 被引用次數 (times-cited count) on each thesis record and lets users trace citations between Taiwanese theses. This is the system where scenarios (a) and (b) are most directly counted. The NCL compiles thesis-to-thesis citation statistics and holds annual 臺灣學術資源影響力 / 能量風貌 events with school-level awards. These are domestic library recognitions, not world rankings, the counting method is not publicly documented in what I found, and some universities dispute the methodology.

### Cited Findings
- The NDLTD manual says each thesis detail page has an "E區：相關次數區" showing 被引用次數 (times cited), 點閱次數 (views), 評分 (ratings), 電子全文下載次數 (full-text downloads) and 被收藏次數 (times bookmarked). — [NCL NDLTD 使用說明 (detail page)](https://ndltd.ncl.edu.tw/gs32/nclcdr/Operating/2-1-3.html); [NDLTD 使用說明](https://ndltd.ncl.edu.tw/gs32/nclcdr/Operating/2-1-1.html). The search summary did not say which of the two pages contains the E區 quote.
- NTU Library: NDLTD "可查詢國內博碩士論文互相引用的情形" (can look up how domestic theses cite each other). TCI-HSS results mark how many times an article was cited by journal literature and by theses. — [NTU Library blog: 如何查詢文章的被引用次數](http://tul.blog.ntu.edu.tw/archives/10819)
- The NCL's analyses include "利用系統統計學位論文間引用情形" (system statistics on citations between theses). NCL reports describe NDLTD as holding 1.23M+ thesis records, about 550k with authorized e-full text, uploaded daily to the international NDLTD Union Archive, where NCL is the second-largest uploader after OCLC. — [NCL 臺灣學術資源能量風貌報告 (2024 PDF)](https://nclfile.ncl.edu.tw/files/202404/051a7e90-9537-4e2d-8fae-3ca4ea2ba945.pdf); [NCL report (2021 PDF)](https://nclfile.ncl.edu.tw/files/202104/7a57101a-40ed-4942-8620-af1ad304c56d.pdf). The search summary did not say which PDF carries which figure.
- NDLTD is a free Ministry of Education project run by the NCL. Bibliographic files are published as open data by academic year; academic year 113 has 59,071 records. — [data.gov.tw dataset 14024](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/14024)
- The 2013 NCL slides argue that theses with online full text are more likely to be cited, as a reason to authorize open full text. — [NCL 2013 presentation](https://lib.hfu.edu.tw/var/file/1/1001/img/55/815064612.pdf)
- NCL award categories have included 學位論文傳播獎, 學位論文開放獎, 學位論文資源貢獻獎, 期刊資源貢獻獎, 期刊即時傳播獎 and 期刊長期傳播獎, based on NDLTD usage data. The search summary did not say which article carried each detail. At the 2024 event (112年 report, held 2024-04-26), Tunghai University's awards were 學位論文開放獎 (by authorization count and by authorization rate), 學位論文取用獎 (by downloads) and 學位論文資源貢獻獎. — [CNA press release, 2024](https://www.cna.com.tw/postwrite/chi/370433)

These are university press releases.
- NTNU says its theses have been cited more than 460k times, first in Taiwan. — [NTNU Alumni Center](https://alumni.ntnu.edu.tw/ActiveMessage/MessageView?itemid=1160&mid=8). This is the university's own claim.
- NTU Library publicly criticized the NCL "學術影響力獎". Without complete data, or for material the system does not cover, the results "無法與大眾的認知完全相符" (cannot fully match public perception). NTU theses are also distributed abroad under non-exclusive licences, so one system cannot reflect their use fully. — [NTU Library statement](http://www.lib.ntu.edu.tw/node/3030)

### Inferences
- A thesis-to-thesis citation, scenario (a) or (b), when both theses are in NDLTD, likely adds to the cited thesis record's 被引用次數. The credit goes to the thesis's author and, in NCL's school-level statistics, to the **degree-granting school**. It does not go to the advisor as a person, or to the professor's current employer when the professor's thesis came from another university. This is an inference from the record-level display and school-level award structure; NCL's method is undocumented.
- Scenario (c), the professor's journal or conference papers, is outside NDLTD's thesis-to-thesis citation tracking.
- NDLTD citation counts feed no world ranking. They show up in NCL recognitions that universities cite in their own publicity.

### Gaps
- How 被引用次數 is computed is undocumented in the sources found: reference-string matching, which theses' references are captured, and whether citations from journals count.
- I found no 2025–2026 award results and could not confirm whether a citation-based award category still exists. The latest data found are from 2023–2024.
- I found no evidence that NDLTD citation counts are used in Ministry of Education evaluations (for example 高教深耕 indicators) or in faculty promotion. Not searched exhaustively.

## 6. Other Taiwanese citation indexes (TCI-HSS, Airiti Citation Index / Airiti Library) and domestic evaluations

### Takeaway
TCI-HSS (built by the NCL, the National Science and Technology Council's humanities and social sciences department and STPI) indexes humanities and social science journal articles, mainly *doctoral* theses, and books as source documents, and shows how often an item is cited by journals and by theses. ACI covers the references of about 700 Taiwanese humanities and social science journals. Airiti Library shows theses among the citing items of journal articles. These are domestic tools (TCI-HSS underpins Taiwan's humanities and social science journal evaluation) and none feeds a world ranking. Both TCI-HSS and ACI cover humanities and social sciences only, so they would not apply to an engineering lab's theses or papers.

### Cited Findings
- TCI-HSS is built by the NCL, the NSTC humanities and social sciences department and STPI. Its source documents are journal articles, doctoral theses and books. Its thesis coverage is mainly 臺灣大專校院人文及社會科學系所之博士論文 (humanities and social science doctoral theses from Taiwanese universities). It records source and cited-reference data and computes citation rates. Results can be sorted by citation count and show how many citations came from journal literature and from theses. — [Feng Chia University Library: TCI-HSS guide](https://web.lib.fcu.edu.tw/library/resources/guides/tci-hss.html); [NTU Library blog: 認識TCI-HSS資料庫與台灣期刊評比指標 THCI/TSSCI](http://tul.blog.ntu.edu.tw/archives/18161); [NTU Library blog 10819](http://tul.blog.ntu.edu.tw/archives/10819); [NCL launch announcement](https://www.ncl.edu.tw/information_236_3137.html). Library guides further describe journal coverage from 2000 (1,000+ peer-reviewed humanities and social science journals published in Taiwan and overseas Chinese-language regions) and book coverage (monographs and proceedings, excluding textbooks, exam guides and popular science). — [PolyU Library: TCI-HSS](https://www.lib.polyu.edu.hk/databases/TCI-HSS); [NQU Library](https://library.nqu.edu.tw/p/404-1004-4481.php?Lang=zh-tw). The search tool noted that guides differ on exact years and on whether master's theses are included; most of these pages are several years old.
- ACI "收錄臺灣人文社會學術期刊之書目資料與參考文獻" (records the bibliographic data and references of Taiwanese humanities and social science journals): about 700 journals, from 1956 onward. Another Airiti figure gives about 400 actively published journals, so the two counts conflict, possibly because they were measured at different times or on different bases.

The search summary did not say which of the two posts made this point.

### Inferences
- A master's thesis citation generally falls outside TCI-HSS's source documents, because thesis coverage is doctoral-focused. It may still appear in Airiti Library's citing-item display for a Taiwanese journal article (scenario c, humanities and social sciences only).
- None of these Taiwanese indexes feeds the global rankings. Their use is domestic: journal evaluation (THCI/TSSCI via TCI-HSS) and library or press-release recognition.

## 7. Attribution nuance: does credit go to the professor or the university, and can an old thesis from another university count for the current employer?

### Takeaway
In Scopus-, WoS- and OpenAlex-based metrics, credit flows through **indexed publications and the affiliation printed on them**. A non-indexed thesis has no record, so nobody is credited for it, including the professor's current employer. Google Scholar is the exception: credit flows through a **self-curated personal profile** whose institution is the person's current one. An old thesis from another university that the professor adds to the profile counts toward that profile, and through Google-Scholar-profile metrics such as Webometrics' Openness (historically), toward the current institution. In NDLTD, credit most plausibly stays with the thesis author and the degree-granting school.

### Cited Findings
- Scopus Author Profiles are built from Scopus-indexed documents, with affiliations taken from document metadata. Users can request a different primary affiliation only from those that appear in their documents. — [Elsevier: Scopus Author Profile FAQs](https://www.elsevier.support/scopus/answer/scopus-author-profile-faqs)
- Scopus affiliation profiles are built from the affiliation text in indexed articles. The automated process "can never be exact", and the Institution Profile Wizard exists so institutions can review it. — [Elsevier: About the Institution Profile Wizard](https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/25554/supporthub/scopus/); [IPW user guide v8](https://supportcontent.elsevier.com/RightNow%20Next%20Gen/Scopus/Files/IPWUserGuidev8.pdf)
- InCites institutional metrics rest on the WoS Core Collection. — [NJIT guide](https://researchguides.njit.edu/wos); [Clarivate LibGuide](https://clarivate.libguides.com/librarianresources/coverage)
- Leiden Open Edition output sets (core and non-core) are made up of journal publications identified in OpenAlex. — [Leiden Open Edition: Indicators](https://open.leidenranking.com/information/indicators); [Leiden Madtrics 2025](https://www.leidenmadtrics.nl/articles/the-cwts-leiden-ranking-2025-more-open-more-inclusive-more-informative)
- Google Scholar profile owners choose which articles appear, and metrics are computed automatically. — [Google Scholar Profiles help](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/citations.html). In 2019 Webometrics filtered Scholar profiles by institutional e-mail domain. — [Webometrics 2019 notes](https://research.webometrics.info/en/current_edition)
- NCL thesis awards and statistics are reported by school. — [CNA 2024](https://www.cna.com.tw/postwrite/chi/370433); [CTEE 2023](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20230511700775-431204)

### Inferences
- Scenario (a): a professor's own master's thesis, possibly decades old and from another university, has no Scopus, WoS Core or OpenAlex-journal record carrying the current employer's affiliation. No ranking can credit it to the current university, regardless of time windows (which the ranking researcher covers). In Google Scholar it can follow the professor if they add it to their profile. In NDLTD it stays attached to the school that awarded it.
- Scenario (b): a lab member's thesis is authored by the student, and its printed affiliation is the university that awarded it, which may be the professor's current university. It is still not an indexed publication anywhere rankings look.
- Scenario (c): the only part of the picture where the current university could gain ranking credit is a citation to the professor's indexed papers. That gain requires the citing document itself to be indexed (Scopus or WoS Core), or possibly an OpenAlex work if Leiden counts non-core citing works, which is unverified. A master's thesis is none of these.

### Gaps
- No source addresses explicitly how a non-indexed thesis could be linked to a current employer. The conclusion is inferred from how profiles and affiliations are built.

## 8. Bottom line: database-by-database answer for scenarios (a), (b), (c)

### Takeaway
A citation printed inside a Taiwanese master's thesis is **not counted in any database that the bibliometric world rankings use** (Scopus/SciVal, WoS Core Collection/InCites, and almost certainly not in practice in OpenAlex/Leiden). It is counted, or plausibly counted, only in **Google Scholar**, which is personal-profile-level, with Webometrics Openness as the one historical ranking link, and in **NDLTD Taiwan**, which is domestic and reflected in NCL school-level recognitions. Some HSS-only domestic indexes add display-level counts.

### Cited Findings
- Scopus does not consider dissertations as books, and citations come only from indexed documents. — [Elsevier content policy](https://www.elsevier.com/products/scopus/content/content-policy-and-selection); [UCL secondary documents](https://library-guides.ucl.ac.uk/scopus/secondary-documents)
- WoS Core Collection Times Cited counts only Core Collection citing publications, and theses sit in a separate index. — [WoS help: Core Collection record](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/26916215746321-Core-Collection-Full-Record-Details); [WoS Coverage Details](https://webofscience.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/48137951657233-Web-of-Science-Coverage-Details)
- The Leiden Open Edition counts only journal publications (core and non-core) as output. — [Leiden Open Edition indicators](https://open.leidenranking.com/information/indicators)
- Google Scholar indexes theses, and much of its extra citation coverage comes from theses and other non-journal sources. — [Google Scholar inclusion](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/inclusion.html); [Martín-Martín et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.05053)
- NDLTD shows 被引用次數 per thesis and tracks citations between theses. — [NDLTD manual](https://ndltd.ncl.edu.tw/gs32/nclcdr/Operating/2-1-3.html); [NTU Library blog](http://tul.blog.ntu.edu.tw/archives/10819)

### Inferences
Matrix (my synthesis of the sections above; "No" means no evidence of any counting mechanism, and the mechanisms found exclude it):

| Database / metric | (a) Professor's own master's thesis | (b) Earlier lab member's thesis (professor = advisor) | (c) Professor's journal/conference papers | Who gets credit, if anything counts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scopus / SciVal (Scopus-based rankings) | No: neither cited nor citing thesis is indexed | No | No: the citing thesis is not indexed, so its references are never harvested | n/a |
| WoS Core Collection / InCites | No | No | No: Core Times Cited counts Core-only citing records | n/a |
| WoS PQDT Citation Index (All Databases) | No evidence Taiwanese theses are in PQDT; outside the Core in any case | Same | Same; at most an All Databases count, never Core/InCites | n/a |
| OpenAlex / Leiden Open Edition | Thesis is never Leiden output (not a journal article); unverified whether TW theses exist in OpenAlex | Same | Only if the citing thesis is an OpenAlex work with references **and** Leiden counts non-core citing works; both unverified, likely negligible | Cited paper's institutions (by printed affiliation) |
| Google Scholar | Plausible: "Cited by" on the thesis record if both are indexed and the reference is parsed; reaches the professor's profile only if they add the thesis | Counts for the student's record; reaches the professor's profile only via advisor mis-parsing or manual (mis)attribution | Plausible: adds to the paper's "Cited by", the professor's profile total and h-index | The person (profile); the institution only through GS-profile-based metrics (Webometrics Openness, current method unconfirmed) |
| NDLTD Taiwan | Likely yes, if both theses are in NDLTD (被引用次數 +1) | Likely yes | No: NDLTD tracks thesis-to-thesis citations | Thesis author; degree-granting school in NCL statistics/awards (inferred) |
| TCI-HSS (HSS only) | Generally no: master's theses are not the main source documents | Generally no | Only if the paper is in a TCI-HSS journal and the citing item is a TCI-HSS source (journal, doctoral thesis, book) | Domestic HSS journal/author metrics |
| ACI / Airiti Library (HSS journals) | Unknown | Unknown | Possibly displayed as a citing item if the paper is in an Airiti/ACI journal and the thesis is in Airiti's ETD collection | Display only, domestic |

- Taken together, inserting citations to the university's professors' master's theses into one's own master's thesis cannot move any Scopus-, WoS- or OpenAlex-based world-ranking indicator. The only effects found are on Google Scholar personal profiles and on NDLTD's domestic thesis-citation counts, and the latter credit the degree-granting school of the *cited thesis*, not necessarily the professor's current university.
