## 1. COPE guidance on citation manipulation: definitions and recommended actions

### Takeaway
COPE's July 2019 discussion document "Citation manipulation" defines manipulative citation as citation behaviour meant to inflate citation counts for personal gain. Its examples are excessive self-citation, excessive citation of the publishing journal, and coordinated citation between journals. COPE treats a citation as a problem when the reference adds nothing to the article's scholarly content and is there only to raise counts. It recommends policies, editor education and response procedures rather than hard caps. COPE case records show editors and reviewers who push citations without a scholarly reason losing their roles.

### Cited Findings
- COPE Council published the discussion document "Citation manipulation" in July 2019 (DOI 10.24318/cope.2019.3.1). — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/discussion-document/citation-manipulation); [INIST summary](https://lalist.inist.fr/citation-manipulation/)
- **Definition:** manipulative citation is behaviour intended to inflate citation counts for personal gain. The examples given are excessive self-citation, excessive citation of the journal that publishes the article, and coordinated excessive citation between journals. — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/discussion-document/citation-manipulation); same definition as summarised by Taiwan's MOE academic-ethics e-paper, Sept 2019: [ethics.moe.edu.tw](https://ethics.moe.edu.tw/files/resource/epaper/epaper_moe_201909.pdf)
- **Core test:** citation manipulation is a problem when references "do not contribute to the scholarly content of the article" and are included solely to increase citations. — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/discussion-document/citation-manipulation); [INIST summary](https://lalist.inist.fr/citation-manipulation/)
- The document also discusses when self-citation or editor-recommended citations can be justified. Self-citation is not banned in itself. — [INIST summary](https://lalist.inist.fr/citation-manipulation/)
- **Recommended actions:** journals should set policies on appropriate levels of self-citation, educate editors, and have clear procedures for responding to suspected manipulation. — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/discussion-document/citation-manipulation); [ethics.moe.edu.tw e-paper](https://ethics.moe.edu.tw/files/resource/epaper/epaper_moe_201909.pdf)
- COPE stops short of universal caps. One summary says imposed caps on author self-citation, on reviewer/editor-suggested citations, or on journal self-citation could actually encourage those practices. The same summary notes that citation indexes have refused or removed journals where the evidence showed clearly intentional self-citation. — [INIST summary](https://lalist.inist.fr/citation-manipulation/)
- **COPE peer-reviewer standard:** reviewers should not suggest citations to their own or associates' work merely to increase citation counts or visibility. Any suggestion "must be based on valid academic or technological reasons." — [COPE case page summarising reviewer guidance](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/case/reviewer-requesting-addition-multiple-citations-their-own-work)
- **COPE case 18-03:** a handling editor and reviewers he or she often invited routinely asked authors to add citations to papers the editor had co-authored. The editorial board found that in many cases the suggested citations "did not advance the argument of the paper." The editor resigned, and the journal introduced editor education and annual ethics affirmations. — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/news-opinion/editor-and-reviewers-requiring-authors-cite-their-own-work)
- **COPE case 19-01:** a reviewer habitually asked authors to cite his own work "often when there was no scientifically legitimate reason to do so," in some cases asking for more than 30 of his own papers in a single manuscript. — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/case/reviewer-requesting-addition-multiple-citations-their-own-work)
- **Prevalence signal:** in a survey of 220 researchers at the US National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences, 22.7% said they had received reviews asking them to add unnecessary references to the reviewer's own publications (as cited in a 2022 arXiv study). — [arXiv 2203.17239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.17239)

### Inferences
- COPE's line is drawn by **relevance and contribution**, not by who wrote the cited work. Citing one's own group is legitimate when the work is actually used. Citation becomes manipulation when the reference is there for the count.
- COPE guidance is addressed mainly to journals, editors and reviewers, but its definition covers authors too: "excessive self-citation … for personal gain," and by extension institutional gain. A student who adds references only to help their university's numbers fits that definition.
- The answer to the user's question is in the definition itself. If a professor's master's thesis is not relevant to the student's argument, citing it "to help the ranking" is the textbook case COPE describes. The ranking intent is the aggravating element, not a justification.

### Gaps
- The full COPE PDF could not be opened, so any numeric thresholds and the exact list of recommended steps are unverified.
- I could not confirm through search the ICMJE wording often quoted as "References should not be used by authors, editors, or peer reviewers to promote self-interests." Verify it at icmje.org before using it.

## 2. Clarivate: JCR title suppressions (2023–2026) and Highly Cited Researchers exclusions (2023–2025)

### Takeaway
Clarivate withholds a journal's Impact Factor for a year when it finds anomalous self-citation or "citation stacking." The yearly count of suppressed journals was 4 (2023), 17 (2024) and 20 (2025). The June 2026 release suppressed further titles, but I could not verify the total. Since 2023, Clarivate's Highly Cited Researchers (HCR) list has screened out a rapidly growing number of candidates for integrity reasons: more than 1,000 in 2023, 2,045 in 2024, and about 2,400 reported for 2025. The screens include anomalous self-citation and "group citation." Clarivate also dropped mathematics in 2023 because of manipulation risk and restored it in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**JCR suppression: rules and consequences**
- Clarivate uses two categories. **Citation stacking** is two or more journals citing each other at an "unusually high rate." **Self-citation** is a journal with an "unusually high percentage" of citations to itself. — [Retraction Watch, 18 Jun 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/18/clarivate-impact-factor-suppression-list-2025-self-citation-stacking/)
- For suppressed titles, the JIF, 5-year JIF and Immediacy Index are not published "due to anomalous citation patterns." Clarivate monitors these titles and restores their metrics in a later release once the patterns are resolved. Web of Science coverage is "not immediately affected." — [Clarivate JCR help: Title Suppressions](https://jcr.help.clarivate.com/Content/title-suppressions.htm); [Zendesk copy](https://journalcitationreports.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/28351398819089-Title-Suppressions); [Clarivate refresher on suppression policy](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/refresher-course-jcr-journal-suppression-policies/)
- One search summary attributes these criteria to Clarivate: the donor journal's citations as a % of the recipient's total, the concentration of exchanged citations in the JIF numerator, a one-year suppression period, and no suppression of new journals. The exact Clarivate page was not identified. — [JCR Reference Guide 2025 (library copy)](https://biblioteka.sum.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/JCR-Reference-Guide-2025.pdf) *(attribution uncertain)*
- **Counts by year:** 33 journals were suppressed for self-citation in the 2020 release; 4 in the 2023 release; 17 in 2024; 20 in 2025 (4 for stacking, the rest for self-citation). — [Retraction Watch, 18 Jun 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/18/clarivate-impact-factor-suppression-list-2025-self-citation-stacking/)
- In 2025 one publisher said its suppression came from being cited by another suppressed journal, *Applied Organometallic Chemistry*, and called the decision "very wrong and dangerous." — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/18/clarivate-impact-factor-suppression-list-2025-self-citation-stacking/)
- *Clinical Hemorheology and Microcirculation* was one of the 20 titles that lost their JIF in 2025, and Sage retracted more than 40 (reported as 45) of its papers. — [Retraction Watch listing](https://retractionwatch.com/category/by-publisher/page/3)
- **2025 rule change:** citations to retracted papers were excluded from the JIF calculation for the first time. About 1% of journals were affected, and none lost a JIF because of it. — [Clarivate blog](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/the-upcoming-journal-citation-reports-release-and-changes-to-uphold-research-integrity-in-2025/); [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/18/clarivate-impact-factor-suppression-list-2025-self-citation-stacking/)
- **2026 release:** JCR 2026 was released on 17 June 2026 with 22,643 journals carrying a JIF (one secondary source says 254 categories). The retracted-citation exclusion continues. — [JournalMetrics](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/jcr-2026-impact-factor-release-guide); [Pitt HSLS](https://info.hsls.pitt.edu/updatereport/august-2026/journal-citation-reports-2026-now-available/); [Clarivate JCR 2026 blog](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/journal-citation-reports-2026-supporting-transparent-responsible-journal-evaluation/)
- Clarivate's suppression page is headed "Journals Suppressed from 2025 JCR Data (2026 release)." The truncated snippet shows titles including *Aestimum*, *Aging Medicine and Healthcare*, *Avances en Ciencias e Ingenieria*, *Balneo and PRM Research Journal* and *International Journal of e-Health and Medical Communications*, plus one listed as "Action Learning." Each is tagged "Stacking" or "Self Citation." — [Clarivate Title Suppressions](https://jcr.help.clarivate.com/Content/title-suppressions.htm)

**Earlier and related Clarivate actions (dated cases)**
- **2013, Brazil "citation cartel":** Thomson Reuters (then owner of the JCR) suspended the impact factors of 14 journals. Four of them were Brazilian journals that had stacked citations to each other: *Clinics*, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, *Revista da Associação Médica Brasileira* and *Acta Ortopédica Brasileira*. From 2009 onward, editors had published articles carrying hundreds of references to each other's journals, which evaded self-citation screens. The *Clinics* editor, Mauricio Rocha-e-Silva, was fired in July 2013. He blamed CAPES, the federal graduate-evaluation agency, for putting too much weight on impact factors. — [Nature, Aug 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/500510a); [Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/punishment-citation-stacking/); [Retraction Watch, 30 Aug 2013](https://retractionwatch.com/2013/08/30/journal-retracts-two-papers-after-being-caught-manipulating-citations)
- **Dec 2023, *Genetika* (Serbian Genetics Society):** the journal got no updated JIF after Clarivate found signs of citation stacking, and it was dropped from Web of Science. It then retracted 31 papers linked to an alleged "Iranian Plant Paper Mill." The notice says that authors and reviewers of the retracted papers, and also "authors who misused the papers by citing them unjustifiably," will be barred from publishing in the journal. — [Retraction Watch, 12 Dec 2023](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/12/12/journal-retracts-31-papers-bans-authors-and-reviewers-after-losing-its-impact-factor/)
- **2026, *International Journal of Surgery*:** a Retraction Watch investigation on 12 Mar 2026 reported that a mandatory requirement to cite reporting guidelines had inflated IJS's impact factor (2022 JIF 15.3, second in surgery). On 19 Mar 2026 Clarivate put six titles from the same publishing group on hold, so new content is not indexed during re-evaluation. On 7 Apr 2026 Wolters Kluwer confirmed it had removed the requirement. No final decision had been reported by late June 2026. — [Retraction Watch, 24 Jun 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/06/24/major-citation-index-put-surgery-journals-on-hold-following-retraction-watch-investigation); [CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/news/clarivate-international-journal-of-surgery-hold)

**Highly Cited Researchers exclusions**
- **2023:** "the number of preliminary candidates excluded from our final list increased from 500 in 2022 to more than 1,000 for 2023," because Clarivate applied more qualitative criteria. Mathematics was excluded from that year's analysis because low publication and citation rates make the field "more vulnerable to strategies to optimize status and rewards through publication and citation manipulation." — [Clarivate HCR 2023 blog](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/highly-cited-researchers-2023-the-evolution-of-our-evaluation-and-selection-policy-to-support-a-robust-scholarly-landscape/)
- **2024:** 2,045 unique individuals were excluded (1,177 in Cross-field, 868 in the other ESI categories). Clarivate also put qualitative-stage exclusions at about 23% of candidates, up from about 4.5% in 2021. — [Clarivate blog](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/the-continuing-evolution-of-the-highly-cited-researchers-list-to-promote-fairness-accuracy-and-research-integrity/); [Clarivate HCR 2025 blog](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/blog/highly-cited-researchers-2025-integrity-drives-selection/) *(which of the two posts carries which figure is uncertain)*
- A CGIAR article says Clarivate cited "concerns around research integrity" for exclusions without asserting misconduct. It gives 2,400 exclusions against 500 in 2022, without stating the year. — [CGIAR](https://www.cgiar.org/news-events/news/why-are-more-researchers-now-screened-out-citations-leaders-list-highly-cited)
- **2025 list (12 Nov 2025):** 7,131 awards to 6,868 individuals at more than 1,300 institutions in 60 countries/regions. Clarivate added checks for hyper-prolific authorship, excessive self-citation and anomalous citation patterns, and says "432 potential awards were excluded due to hyper-prolific publication." — [Clarivate press release](https://clarivate.com/news/clarivate-announces-highly-cited-researchers-2025-list/)
- Retraction Watch reports that about 2,400 researchers were excluded in 2025 for integrity concerns, 432 of them for hyper-prolific authorship. Exclusions rose from 500 (2022) to 1,000 (2023) to over 2,000 (2024). The criteria include anomalous self-citation or **group citation**, authorship of papers retracted for integrity reasons, community reports of integrity breaches, and an anomalous ratio of reviews to articles. The criteria are now applied consistently to all researchers; earlier they were applied manually to particular regions or disciplines (Clarivate's Dmytro Filchenko). The usual retention rate of about 70% fell to about 60%. Mathematics returned after a two-year suppression. — [Retraction Watch, 12 Nov 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/11/12/math-is-back-as-clarivate-boosts-integrity-markers-in-highly-cited-researchers-list/)
- For 2025, Clarivate also removed Highly Cited Papers authored by individuals excluded from the 2024 list for integrity concerns. Selection starts from about 200,000 top-1% papers; retracted papers and those with more than 30 authors are removed, leaving about 9,000 authors, who are then screened. Clarivate says "human qualitative evaluation remains an important part of our selection process." — [Clarivate HCR 2025 analysis](https://clarivate.com/highly-cited-researchers/analysis/); [Fritz Haber Institute](https://www.fhi.mpg.de/2167843/2025-11-17_Clarivate-Ranking)

### Inferences
- Penalties and screening have intensified sharply since 2023: JIF suppressions went from 4 to 20 per year, HCR exclusions from about 500 to about 2,400, and penalties now spread to co-authors and to papers. The same tools that build rankings now actively look for self-referential citation clusters.
- The Genetika notice and the HCR "group citation" screen show that **citing authors**, not only journals, can be penalised for citations without a scholarly reason.
- Several ranking indicators draw on these Clarivate products (HCR feeds ARWU, for example). Gaming them therefore risks the institution losing credit, not gaining it.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the total number of journals suppressed in the June 2026 JCR release, or the full list.
- The 2025 HCR exclusion total is reported as about 2,400 by Retraction Watch and CGIAR. Clarivate's own release, as captured, gives only the 432 hyper-prolific figure. The 2024 figure of 2,045 is Clarivate's.
- No Taiwanese journals or researchers were identified in the suppression or exclusion lists in the material retrieved. Clarivate does not publish the names of excluded HCR candidates.

## 3. Ranking agencies' responses and documented cases of institutions gaming rankings

### Takeaway
THE reserves the right to exclude institutions for deliberate data manipulation. It screens with Elsevier for integrity red flags such as retractions, self-citation and discontinued journals. On 30 Sept 2026 it announced that the 2028 World University Rankings will **cap institutional self-citations**. QS already excludes author-level self-citations from its citation indicator. Documented gaming has mostly targeted affiliations and citation networks rather than individual students' references. Examples include Saudi universities paying Highly Cited Researchers for affiliations, Spanish scientists sanctioned for taking part, mathematics citation cartels, the Saveetha self-citation scheme in India, and Italy's country-level citation gaming after 2011. The consequences include dismissal-level sanctions, ranking drops and removal from lists.

### Cited Findings
**Times Higher Education (THE)**
- THE's corrections policy says that if a university's data was deliberately manipulated, THE "reserves the right to exclude the institution from future rankings, remove them from historical rankings, and publish a note." — [THE corrections policy](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/times-higher-education-world-university-rankings-corrections-policy)
- THE's post "Protecting the integrity of university rankings" (date not captured; it refers to the 2026 edition's Scopus base of 18 million outputs and 170 million citations) makes several points:
  - THE works with Elsevier to screen ranked universities for red flags: unusually high shares of retracted articles, of self-citations, or of publications in discontinued journals. Outliers can be pulled out for investigation.
  - THE does not use journal-based metrics such as the JIF.
  - Papers per faculty carries 5.5% weight, against 30% for quality and impact measures.
  - The post cites Meho's Research Integrity Risk Index.
  - THE had not then decided to ban self-citations, calling full removal "too blunt."
  — [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/739285)
- **30 Sept 2026:** THE said it will make "modest" changes for the **2028** WUR (due autumn 2027). The first listed change is **capping institutional self-citations used in bibliometric analyses**; research quality metrics will also be adjusted. The changes are to be formally confirmed next year. — [THE, "Metric modifications will help maintain rankings quality"](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/744136)
- An earlier THE masterclass Q&A said self-citations were counted in the citation score and not excluded. — [THE Q&A (India)](https://timeshighereducation.com/sites/default/files/2024_data_masterclass_qa_-_india.pdf)
- In 2020, CESAER, a network of European science and technology universities, wrote to THE urging it to use citation data without author self-citations. — [CESAER letter, 8 May 2020](https://www.cesaer.org/content/5-operations/2020/20200508-letter-to-the-on-world-university-rankings-signed.pdf)
- THE has reported a scholar's retraction-adjusted ranking, which penalises retractions, excessive self-citation and papers in discontinued journals. In that analysis Saudi Arabia, China, Malaysia, Iran, India and Indonesia carry penalties that on average exceed their number of top-cited authors. — [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/740695)

**QS**
- QS's Citations per Faculty indicator (20% weight, Scopus data, 6 years of citations to papers from a 5-year window) **excludes self-citations at the author level**. — [QS support: Citations per Faculty](https://support.qs.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019107580-Citations-per-Faculty); [QS: Processing of citations and papers](https://support.qs.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4411823040018-Processing-of-citations-and-papers)
- From the QS 2025 edition, excluded citations also include those linked to excluded document types and those affected by the affiliation cap. — [Elsevier SciVal: Citation Count (QS)](https://www.elsevier.support/scival/answer/citation-count)
- QS removed South Korea's Chung-Ang University from its 2018 assessment for data manipulation (June 2017). This concerned institutional data, not citations. — [Korea Times, 14 Jun 2017](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/society/20170614/s-koreas-chung-ang-university-cut-from-qs-rankings-for-data-manipulation)
- When Yonsei and Korea University were reported to have hired overseas scholars with little engagement to boost QS/THE positions, a QS official said its screening flags fluctuations in staff numbers and requires evidence for unusually large changes. The article is undated and the outcome was not found. — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/global-university-rankings-provider-may-probe-south-koreas-yonsei-korea-university-over-malpractice-claims/)

**Saudi universities and Highly Cited Researcher affiliations**
- A 2014 analysis found King Abdulaziz University (KAU) had 11 HCRs listing it as primary affiliation and 133 listing it as secondary, which put it first on the secondary-affiliation list, ahead of Harvard. — [Bornmann & Bauer, arXiv 1408.6438](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.6438)
- Yves Gingras found that 82% of Saudi-linked HCRs (139 of 170) listed Saudi Arabia only as a secondary affiliation. He linked this to a 2011 *Science* investigation into lucrative adjunct contracts that required little presence. — [THE, 2014](https://timeshighereducation.com/node/122976)
- In SIRIS's analysis of 2014–2022 HCR data, Saudi primary-affiliated HCRs rose from 27 to 109. There were 44 research-fellowship-only affiliations, mainly at King Saud University and KAU, and many Saudi-affiliated HCRs listed Spanish institutions as secondary. ARWU counts only primary affiliations. — [SIRIS](https://www.sirisacademic.com/blog/the-affiliation-game-of-saudi-arabian-higher-education-research-institutions); [SIRIS (Spain–Saudi)](https://sirisacademic.com/blog/the-affiliation-game-between-spanish-and-saudi-arabian-higher-education-research-institutions)

**Spain: sanctions for participating**
- On 18 Apr 2023 *El País* (Manuel Ansede) reported that more than a dozen Spanish academics had falsely listed Saudi institutions as their primary HCR affiliation. Chemist Mira Petrovic said a Saudi university had offered her €70,000 a year to do so. — [El País via IIUM newsroom](https://newsroom.iium.edu.my/index.php/2023/04/20/saudi-arabia-pays-spanish-scientists-to-pump-up-global-university-rankings/)
- Chemist **Rafael Luque** switched his primary affiliation to King Saud University in 2019 without telling his employer. The University of Córdoba suspended him from employment and salary for **13 years**, reported 31 Mar 2023 and described as a de facto dismissal. Per a SIRIS report cited by *El País*, Córdoba fell about 150 places in ARWU. Luque disputes the university's account. — [Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/prolific-spanish-chemist-suspended-over-multiple-affiliations/4017322.article); [Diari de Tarragona](https://www.diaridetarragona.com/internacional/185058/suspenden-13-anos-de-empleo-y-sueldo-a-un-cientifico-espanol-ip14394792.html); [SIRIS](https://sirisacademic.com/blog/the-affiliation-game-between-spanish-and-saudi-arabian-higher-education-research-institutions); [Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/autor-de-mais-de-700-artigos-e-suspenso-por-13-anos-por-infracao-contratual/)

**Mathematics citation cartels (2023–2024)**
- *Science* (Michele Catanzaro, Jan/Feb 2024) reported "cliques of mathematicians at institutions in China, Saudi Arabia, and elsewhere" inflating colleagues' citation counts with low-quality papers that repeatedly cite each other, according to Domingo Docampo's (University of Vigo) analysis of about 15 years of Clarivate data. These institutions now produce more highly cited mathematics papers per year than Stanford or Princeton. Clarivate had dropped mathematics from HCR in November 2023. — [Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/citation-cartels-help-some-mathematicians-and-their-universities-climb-rankings); [University World News, Feb 2024](https://www.universityworldnews.com/post.php?story=2024020406243096); [ENS copy of article](http://openscience.ens.fr/ABOUT_OPEN_ACCESS/ARTICLES/2024_02_02_Science_Citation_manipulation_by_citation_cartels_in_mathematics.pdf); [Safón, Docampo & Cram 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05781)

**India**
- **Saveetha Dental College (Chennai), June 2023:** about 500 undergraduates a year write a 1,500-word manuscript as part of an exam, contributing to more than 1,400 publications in a year. These papers systematically cited Saveetha faculty work, "including papers on completely unrelated topics," which dramatically inflated citations. The college topped QS's most-cited list in its field two years running and ranked 13th among dental schools. Officials denied any concerted effort. — [Retraction Watch/Science, 8 Jun 2023](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/06/08/did-a-nasty-publishing-scheme-help-an-indian-dental-school-win-high-rankings); [Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/student-articles-used-to-increase-citations-of-colleges-own-papers/)
- In 2024 Saveetha was accused again: scholars allegedly published AI-written commentaries that heavily cited other Saveetha scholars (Wikipedia, secondary source). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saveetha_Institute_of_Medical_and_Technical_Sciences)
- **Research Integrity Risk Index (RI², Lokman Meho, AUB, 2025):** the index uses retraction rates and the share of output in journals later delisted from Scopus or WoS; a later edition adds citations to retracted papers. One report puts 124 institutions in the red-flag group, about a quarter of them in India, 17% in Saudi Arabia and 15% in China. Graphic Era University topped the June 2025 list, with nine of the top ten in India. A 2026 edition reports 37 Indian and 18 Saudi red-flag universities. The author says RI² measures structural risk, not proven misconduct. — [Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/progress-on-shaky-ground/); [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/india-dominates-global-research-integrity-risk-rankings-as-nine-of-top-ten-institutions-flag-severe-publishing-violations); [Morocco World News, Aug 2026](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/08/333484/moroccan-universities-face-research-integrity-concerns-in-new-global-index/); [arXiv 2505.06448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06448); [RI² site](https://sites.aub.edu.lb/lmeho/ri2)

**Italy (country-level gaming)**
- After 2011 rules tied Italian career steps to bibliometric thresholds, Italy became the European country with the highest "inwardness," meaning the share of a country's citations that come from its own authors. The authors attribute this to "a generalized strategic use of citations," both self-citation and citation clubs. — [Baccini, De Nicolao & Petrovich 2019, arXiv/PLOS ONE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.04708); [THE commentary](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/681556)

### Inferences
- From 2028, THE will cap institutional self-citations, meaning a university's own authors citing the university's papers. QS already strips author self-citations. Both are moving to discount exactly the "cite our own people" strategy.
- Documented penalties have fallen on adults and institutions: a 13-year suspension, ranking drops, HCR removal and loss of list categories. No case was found of a student penalised for a single thesis's references. Even so, the norm is the same, and student exam papers were the mechanism in the Saveetha case.

### Gaps
- I found no THE or QS announcement naming universities excluded specifically for citation anomalies, and no formal QS policy penalising citation manipulation.
- The date of THE's "Protecting the integrity of university rankings" post and the size and mechanics of the 2028 cap are not yet published or captured.
- I could not verify reports that Vel Tech (India) rose in THE's 2017 tables because of one researcher's self-citations, so this is omitted.

## 4. Elsevier/Scopus and publisher actions; researchers sanctioned for citation pushing

### Takeaway
Scopus discontinues titles through its Content Selection & Advisory Board (CSAB) re-evaluation and its "Radar" outlier detection; "Publication Concerns," which can include citation anomalies, is the most common stated reason. Publishers have removed editors and banned reviewers for coercive citation. The clearest cases are Kuo-Chen Chou at the *Journal of Theoretical Biology* (2020), an unnamed reviewer banned by *Bioinformatics* (2019), Artemi Cerdà at *Geoderma* (2017), and Elsevier's 2019 investigation of hundreds of reviewers.

### Cited Findings
- **Scopus re-evaluation:** underperforming titles, or titles about which concerns have been raised, are identified through CSAB re-evaluation, and their ongoing coverage is discontinued; they are added to the public "Discontinued Sources" list. OMICS journals were removed in 2017 for "Publication Concerns." — [Retraction Watch, 27 Mar 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/03/27/multiple-omics-journals-delisted-major-index-concerns/)
- Scopus's "Radar" flags unexpected patterns in publication activity, citation graphs and author networks (secondary guide). — [CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/guides/why-and-how-scopus-delists-journals)
- A 2026 study of 62 discontinued journals found the most common grounds were "Publication Concerns" (59.7%) and "Outlier" conduct (14.5%). The authors observed citation stacking that "does not make sense" among the flagged patterns. — [Jurnal FIP, Universitas Negeri Malang](https://journal3.um.ac.id/index.php/fip/article/view/7988)
- **Elsevier reviewer investigation, Sept 2019:** Elsevier analysts Jeroen Baas and Catriona Fennell examined about 55,000 Elsevier reviewers (SSRN preprint, 6 Sept 2019). Fewer than 1% consistently had their own work cited in papers they reviewed, and Elsevier said it was investigating hundreds of researchers suspected of coercive citation. Elsevier said it would not retract papers affected only by coercive citation because the authors were not responsible. — [Nature Index, Sept 2019](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/news/elsevier-investigates-hundreds-peer-reviewers-for-manipulating-citations)
- **Artemi Cerdà (2017):** this University of Valencia soil scientist resigned from the editorial board of *Geoderma* after accusations that he used his position to increase citations to his own work. An investigation by the European Geosciences Union (EGU) and Elsevier confirmed misconduct. — [Nature Index](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/news/elsevier-investigates-hundreds-peer-reviewers-for-manipulating-citations)
- **Kuo-Chen Chou (2020):** the *Journal of Theoretical Biology* (Elsevier) removed Chou from its editorial board after finding he had asked authors of papers he handled to cite his papers, at times more than 50. He also asked them to add the name of his algorithm to their paper titles. The JTB editorial in late January 2020 called coercive citation "misconduct of the highest order." Nature reported in February 2020 that Chou had been barred as a reviewer by several computational biology journals. — [Editage](https://editage.com/insights/elsevier-removes-editor-from-board-after-revelations-of-citation-manipulation); [Nature, Feb 2020](https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-020-00335-7/d41586-020-00335-7.pdf); [Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/crackdown-on-fraudulent-citations/)
- ***Bioinformatics* (OUP), Feb 2019:** the journal banned an unnamed reviewer who requested about 35 extra citations per review, about 90% of them to the reviewer's own papers, with wording implying the recommendation depended on it. OUP's legal department opposed naming the reviewer. Some secondary summaries link this pattern to Chou; treat that link as unverified. — [Retraction Watch, 7 Feb 2019](https://retractionwatch.com/2019/02/07/the-case-of-the-reviewer-who-said-cite-me-or-i-wont-recommend-acceptance-of-your-work/); [case report, PMC6748764](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6748764/)
- ***AIP Advances*, Aug 2024:** the publisher said it would retract an article that cited one researcher in 53 of its 64 references. Six of those citations had been added at a reviewer's request, but the publisher found "no clear evidence of coercion." The paper was retracted for excessive citation of one person. — [Retraction Watch, 14 Aug 2024](https://retractionwatch.com/2024/08/14/exclusive-publisher-to-retract-article-for-excessively-citing-one-researcher-after-retraction-watch-inquiry)

### Inferences
- Publishers mostly sanction the person who **demands** the citation (editor or reviewer). The *AIP Advances* and *Genetika* cases (section 2) show that the **citing paper or its authors** can also be retracted or barred when citations are plainly unjustified.
- Scopus and Elsevier's growing outlier screening means artificial citation clusters are detectable at both journal and institution level. THE's red-flag screening draws on the same Scopus data.

### Gaps
- No Elsevier or Scopus discontinuation notice naming "citation manipulation" for a specific title was captured. Scopus's public discontinuation reasons are coarse categories.
- No Taiwan-affiliated editor or reviewer sanctioned for coercive citation was found.

## 5. Taiwan: regulations, MOE ethics-centre guidance, and Taiwanese cases

### Takeaway
Neither the MOE's rules for handling academic-ethics cases at colleges and universities (專科以上學校學術倫理案件處理原則, 2017) nor the NSTC's case-handling rules (學術倫理案件處理及審議要點, amended 6 Feb 2026) lists "irrelevant or gratuitous citation to boost metrics" as a named type of misconduct. The named types centre on 造假 (fabrication), 變造 (falsification), 抄襲 (plagiarism), 代寫 (ghost-writing), 重複發表 (duplicate publication), 未適當引註 (improper attribution) and, in NSTC's list, 以不當手段影響論文審查 (improperly influencing peer review). NSTC adds a catch-all for other conduct its ethics review board decides on. The MOE's own academic-ethics e-paper (Sept 2019) adopts COPE's definition of 引用操縱 (citation manipulation) and says it is not yet regarded as serious academic fraud but damages credibility. In Taiwan, gratuitous citation is therefore best described as a questionable research practice, which can become misconduct when it involves coercion in peer review or is judged under the catch-all. Taiwan's best-known citation-ring case is the 2014 SAGE *Journal of Vibration and Control* scandal, which led to the Education Minister's resignation.

### Cited Findings
**MOE 專科以上學校學術倫理案件處理原則**
- Issued by the MOE on 31 May 2017 (臺教高(五)字第1060059470號), effective immediately. Alongside it, the MOE amended the teacher-qualification rules and the Teachers' Act so that serious ethics violations became grounds for suspension or dismissal. — [MOE law portal](https://edu.law.moe.gov.tw/NewsContent.aspx?id=4468); [Control Yuan case table](https://cybsbox.cy.gov.tw/CYBSBoxSSL/edoc/additional/download/4165)
- **Listed violation types:**
  - (一) 造假 (fabrication)
  - (二) 變造 (falsification)
  - (三) 抄襲 (plagiarism): "援用他人之申請資料、研究資料或研究成果未註明出處。註明出處不當，情節重大者，以抄襲論" — using others' material without citing the source; seriously improper citation counts as plagiarism
  - (四) 由他人代寫 (ghost-writing)
  - (五) 未經註明而重複出版公開發行 (undisclosed duplicate publication)
  - (六) 大幅引用自己已發表之著作，未適當引註 (extensive reuse of one's own published work without proper citation)
  — [Tunghai University copy](https://upto.thu.edu.tw/upload/upto/sitemap/%E5%B0%88%E7%A7%91%E4%BB%A5%E4%B8%8A%E5%AD%B8%E6%A0%A1%E5%AD%B8%E8%A1%93%E5%80%AB%E7%90%86%E6%A1%88%E4%BB%B6%E8%99%95%E7%90%86%E5%8E%9F%E5%89%87.pdf); [NCCU copy](https://ord.nccu.edu.tw/files/archive/1867_7967275d.pdf)
- Some reproduced versions also list (七) 以翻譯代替論著，並未適當註明 (presenting a translation as original work without disclosure) and (八) 教師資格審查履歷表、合著人證明登載不實 (false statements in teacher-qualification CVs or co-author certifications). I could not confirm whether these are in the MOE text or in teacher-qualification and school-level rules. — [same search result set](https://ord.nccu.edu.tw/files/archive/1866_356953ec.pdf) *(uncertain)*
- **Degree cases** are handled under the Degree Conferral Act (學位授予法), teacher-qualification cases under the teacher-qualification rules, and other cases by schools. Gift or guest authors are not deemed in violation as such, but must bear responsibility if they benefited, for example through a degree, qualification or grant. Advisors can bear responsibility for inadequate supervision (監督不周) of students' theses. — [Tunghai University copy](https://upto.thu.edu.tw/upload/upto/sitemap/%E5%B0%88%E7%A7%91%E4%BB%A5%E4%B8%8A%E5%AD%B8%E6%A0%A1%E5%AD%B8%E8%A1%93%E5%80%AB%E7%90%86%E6%A1%88%E4%BB%B6%E8%99%95%E7%90%86%E5%8E%9F%E5%89%87.pdf); [NTUE copy](https://orad.ntue.edu.tw/var/file/11/1011/img/637/229011166.pdf)
- **Degree Conferral Act (學位授予法) art. 17** (2018 revision): a degree must be revoked and the certificate cancelled if the thesis, work or report involves 造假、變造、抄襲、代寫等舞弊 (fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, ghost-writing or similar fraud). The supervising authority can order corrections when a school mishandles a case. — [6laws](https://6laws.net/6law/laws/學位授予法.html); [YZU copy of 28 Nov 2018 text](https://www.yzu.edu.tw/admin/rd/files/%E5%AD%B8%E5%80%AB%E5%B0%88%E5%8D%80/%E5%AD%B8%E4%BD%8D%E6%8E%88%E4%BA%88%E6%B3%951071128.pdf)

**NSTC (國科會)**
- The NSTC's 學術倫理案件處理及審議要點 was amended on **6 Feb 2026** (科會誠字第1150010385B號令). Point 3 defines violations as:
  - 造假 (fabrication)
  - 變造 (falsification)

**Taiwanese cases and context**
- **2014 SAGE *Journal of Vibration and Control* "peer review and citation ring":** SAGE announced on 8 July 2014 the retraction of 60 JVC papers after a 14-month investigation centred on Peter Chen (Chen Chen-yuan, 陳震遠) of National Pingtung University of Education. He was listed as a co-author on five of the 60 papers, he denied involvement, and the Ministry of Science and Technology later said he was not involved in the fraud. - **Context:** in October 2024 NTU's vice president told the university council that NTU's paper-citation scores had declined in recent QS and THE results, and that NTU would strengthen international ties and paper quality. — [CNA, 19 Oct 2024](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/aall/202410190041.aspx)

### Inferences
- **Misconduct or questionable practice?** Under Taiwan's formal rules, adding irrelevant citations is **not a listed misconduct type**. The listed citation offences all concern **under-citation**: failing to cite others (plagiarism) or one's own prior work (self-plagiarism). Gratuitous **over-citation** sits in the questionable-research-practice zone that the MOE e-paper describes as "not yet serious fraud." It could become a sanctionable case in three ways:
  - (a) it is done through coercion in peer review (NSTC: 以不當手段影響論文審查);
  - (b) an ethics board treats it under NSTC's catch-all (八);
  - (c) for a thesis, the examining committee or advisor rejects it as poor scholarship, a quality matter rather than art. 17 revocation.
- Taiwan's rules **require** students to cite relevant prior work from their own lab or department when they build on it. Not doing so risks 抄襲 or 自我抄襲 findings. Citing the lab's genuinely relevant theses is therefore both allowed and obligatory.
- Advisors carry supervisory responsibility (監督不周). An advisor who encourages a student to add irrelevant departmental citations for ranking purposes exposes themselves, not only the student.

### Gaps
- I could not open the official MOE and NSTC full texts; the article wording comes from university re-postings and search summaries. Whether the MOE principle has been amended since 31 May 2017, and its exact (七)/(八) items, is unverified.
- I found no MOE, NSTC or university document that explicitly addresses "citing for metrics or rankings" in theses, and no Taiwanese case of a student or researcher sanctioned for gratuitous citations.
- China Medical University (Taiwan) may appear in Docampo's and *Science*'s 2024 mathematics-citation data as a producer of highly cited math papers, but the snippet was garbled. **Treat this as unverified** and do not assert it without reading the original.

## 6. Where the line lies: citing relevant own-lab or department work versus inserting irrelevant citations

### Takeaway
The test across COPE, publisher practice, citation-style manuals and Taiwanese thesis norms is the same: **cite a work because your thesis uses it**, as background, method, data, comparison or argument, and place the citation where it is used. Own-lab and own-department citations are normal and often required. Citations added because of who wrote them or whose metrics they help are manipulation. Detection tools increasingly flag self-referential clusters (group citation, inwardness, institutional self-citation caps).

### Cited Findings
- COPE: citations are problematic when they "do not contribute to the scholarly content of the article." — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/discussion-document/citation-manipulation)
- COPE case 18-03: the board's test was whether suggested citations "advance the argument of the paper." — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/news-opinion/editor-and-reviewers-requiring-authors-cite-their-own-work)
- COPE reviewer guidance: citation suggestions "must be based on valid academic or technological reasons," not on boosting counts or visibility. — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/case/reviewer-requesting-addition-multiple-citations-their-own-work)
- **Self-citation is not wrong in itself.** In Nature's August 2019 report on the Ioannidis et al. Scopus-based database, the median self-citation rate (self plus co-authors) was 12.7%. At least 250 scientists drew more than 50% of their citations from themselves or co-authors; the extreme case was 94%. Ioannidis said rates above 25% are "not necessarily" unethical but merit closer scrutiny, and researchers warned against policing self-citation. — [Nature, Aug 2019](https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-019-02479-7/d41586-019-02479-7.pdf); [PLOS Biology, Ioannidis et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000384); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/some-of-the-world-s-most-cited-scientists-have-a-secret-that-s-just-been-exposed)
- **APA guidance** (7th edition, as summarised by library guides): avoid both under-citation, which leads to plagiarism, and over-citation, which is "distracting and unnecessary." For most papers, cite one or two sources per key point; literature reviews are more exhaustive. — [TC3 library FAQ](https://tc3.libanswers.com/faq/387401); [Stonehill guide](https://libguides.stonehill.edu/bus335/apa)
- **Taiwanese thesis norm:** works cited in the text must appear in the reference list, and works not cited in the text must not be listed (正文引用的文獻需列舉在文末參考文獻中，未引用於正文者，文末不得列出). — [Tamkang University, Dept. of Educational Psychology thesis citation rules](https://www.edpsy.tku.edu.tw/File/Archive/A8-15-38-48-BF-C1-EC-05-5B-B9-26-19-AE-30-06-2A.pdf)
- **Taiwanese rules on under-citing one's own work:** the MOE lists 大幅引用自己已發表之著作，未適當引註 as a violation, and the NSTC lists 自我抄襲. — [Tunghai copy of MOE principle](https://upto.thu.edu.tw/upload/upto/sitemap/%E5%B0%88%E7%A7%91%E4%BB%A5%E4%B8%8A%E5%AD%B8%E6%A0%A1%E5%AD%B8%E8%A1%93%E5%80%AB%E7%90%86%E6%A1%88%E4%BB%B6%E8%99%95%E7%90%86%E5%8E%9F%E5%89%87.pdf); [NCU copy of NSTC rules](https://ncu.edu.tw/ori/storage/files/dvWXYKHvuLjVBaod3oyQUOA4S88iW6Wkvawp7RoS.pdf)
- **Paradigm of the wrong side of the line:** Saveetha students' papers cited institutional work "including papers on completely unrelated topics." — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/06/08/did-a-nasty-publishing-scheme-help-an-indian-dental-school-win-high-rankings)
- **Consequences for citing authors:** *Genetika* barred "authors who misused the papers by citing them unjustifiably." *AIP Advances* retracted a paper that cited one researcher in 53 of 64 references. — [Retraction Watch, Dec 2023](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/12/12/journal-retracts-31-papers-bans-authors-and-reviewers-after-losing-its-impact-factor/); [Retraction Watch, Aug 2024](https://retractionwatch.com/2024/08/14/exclusive-publisher-to-retract-article-for-excessively-citing-one-researcher-after-retraction-watch-inquiry)
- **How clusters are defined and detected:**
  - **Citation cartels** are "groups of authors that cite each other disproportionately more than they do other groups of authors that work on the same subject." — [Fister, Fister & Perc 2016, Frontiers in Physics](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphy.2016.00049/full)
  - The **"inwardness"** indicator can flag national citation clubs. — [Baccini et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.04708)
  - Clarivate's HCR screens anomalous **group citation**. — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/11/12/math-is-back-as-clarivate-boosts-integrity-markers-in-highly-cited-researchers-list/)
  - THE will cap **institutional self-citations** from the 2028 WUR. — [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/744136)
  - QS excludes author self-citations. — [QS](https://support.qs.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019107580-Citations-per-Faculty)

### Inferences
- **A practical test a student can apply** (my synthesis of the sources above):
  - (1) Did I actually read and use this work: its method, data, result, design or argument?
  - (2) Would I cite it if the same work had been written at another university?
  - (3) Is the citation attached to the specific sentence or section where I use it, not dumped into an unrelated list?
  - (4) Could I explain to my oral-defence committee why it is there?
  - (5) Does it replace or duplicate a more appropriate primary source?
  
  If the answer to (1)–(4) is yes, citing a lab-mate's or professor's thesis is legitimate and expected. If the citation exists "for the ranking," it falls within COPE's definition of manipulation.
- **Ranking mechanics** (detail is covered by other researchers): citations count in THE and QS only when the citing and cited items are Scopus-indexed documents. QS removes author self-citations, and THE will cap institutional self-citations from 2028. A master's thesis citing a professor's master's thesis is therefore very unlikely to move a world ranking even before ethics is considered.
- Over-citation also has a quality cost. Examiners and journal reviewers read padded reference lists as weak scholarship (APA's "distracting and unnecessary"), which hurts the student's own evaluation.

### Gaps
- There is no single authoritative numeric threshold for "excessive" self-, group- or institutional citation. COPE declines to set caps, and Clarivate's thresholds are not public.
- I could not verify the ICMJE statement on references and self-interest; see section 1.

## 7. Legitimate actions a Taiwanese master's student can take that affect institutional bibliometrics

### Takeaway
What moves Scopus or WoS-based bibliometrics is **indexed publications correctly credited to the university**, and those publications being genuinely read and cited. In practice:
- turn thesis results, with the advisor, into journal or conference papers indexed in Scopus or WoS;
- write the university's official English name exactly as the school prescribes (Taiwanese universities issue affiliation-writing rules precisely so that papers are counted);
- keep a clean author identity (ORCID, and the Scopus Author Feedback Wizard for one's own profile; the Institution Profile Wizard is reserved for authorised university staff);
- make the work findable (open access, repository and thesis deposit).

### Cited Findings
**Why affiliation matters**
- THE uses Elsevier's Scopus as its sole bibliometric source, about 18 million outputs and 170 million citations for the 2026 edition. QS's citation indicator also uses Scopus. — [PR Newswire (THE–Elsevier)](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/new-multi-year-partnership-sees-times-higher-education-continue-to-draw-on-elseviers-scopus-data-for-669879013.html); [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/739285); [QS](https://support.qs.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019107580-Citations-per-Faculty)
- Scopus builds institution profiles automatically from affiliation text, and "this automated process can never be exact." Authorised institutional staff, normally no more than three per institution and named by signed authorisation, can use the **Institution Profile Wizard (IPW)** to fix the hierarchy and preferred name, link articles, or request profiles. Processing takes from a few days to six weeks depending on the source. — [Elsevier: How to use the IPW](https://www.elsevier.support/scopus/answer/how-do-i-use-the-institution-profile-wizard); [Elsevier: About the IPW](https://www.elsevier.support/scopus/answer/about-the-institution-profile-wizard); [Elsevier Connect](https://www.elsevier.com/connect/three-tips-for-managing-scopus-organization-profiles-for-complex)
- Individual authors can request corrections to their own Scopus author profile (affiliation, merging split profiles) through the **Author Feedback Wizard**. A Scopus account is needed, and processing generally takes 2–4 weeks per one library. — [Elsevier: Author Feedback Wizard](https://www.elsevier.support/scopus/answer/how-do-i-use-the-author-feedback-wizard); [Erasmus University library guide](https://libguides.eur.nl/CheckingScopusID)

**Taiwanese university affiliation-writing rules (examples)**
- **Kaohsiung Medical University:** a standard form is set so the university can count the system's total paper output. The university name ("Kaohsiung Medical University") must always be written. In English, units run from the lowest level up to the university, with city and country added. If a journal limits length, keep the lowest unit and the university name. — [KMU principles](https://devel.kmu.edu.tw/images/Div_AR/1015005/general/高雄醫學大學教師發表研究論文作者任職單位中英文名稱撰寫原則.pdf)
- **Taipei Medical University:** the top-level unit "Taipei Medical University" must always be written so the university's papers are counted accurately. English unit names follow the Office of International Affairs' list. Papers that do not follow the rules are **not counted for publication awards, flexible salary or promotion**. — [TMU library policy](https://library.tmu.edu.tw/libfiles/submission-unit/File1_department_policy.pdf); [TMU College of Medicine](https://medicine-en.tmu.edu.tw/?p=17664)
- **Da-Yeh University:** the country must be written as "Taiwan" or "R.O.C." Affiliations written as "China" or "Taiwan, China" are not recognised. — [DYU rules](https://reg.dyu.edu.tw/file/1860.pdf)
- **Chinese Culture University:** city names use Taiwan's customary romanisation (Taipei, Taichung, Kaohsiung), not Hanyu Pinyin. — [PCCU](https://ord.pccu.edu.tw/var/file/8/1008/img/2863/782987172.pdf)
- **National Yunlin University of Science and Technology** has bilingual "Guidelines for Listing Institutional Affiliation and Country Names in Academic Journal Publications in International or Mainland China Journals." — [YunTech](https://admin5.yuntech.edu.tw/~ttx2021_6k24h/images/content/regulations/B_Research/B11%E6%9C%AC%E6%A0%A1%E6%95%99%E5%B8%AB%E7%99%BC%E8%A1%A8%E5%9C%8B%E9%9A%9B%E6%88%96%E5%A4%A7%E9%99%B8%E5%9C%B0%E5%8D%80%E5%AD%B8%E8%A1%93%E6%9C%9F%E5%88%8A%E8%AB%96%E6%96%87%E5%88%97%E5%90%8D%E8%99%95%E7%90%86%E5%8E%9F%E5%89%87Guidelines%20for%20Listing%20Institutional%20Affiliation%20and%20Country%20Names%20in%20Academic%20Journal%20Publications%20in%20International%20or%20Mainland%20China%20Journals.pdf)
- **NYCU:** after the 2021 merger, thesis templates require the official English name "National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University." — [NYCU thesis template](https://dpeecs.nycu.edu.tw/df_ufiles/092/學位論文格式範本-光電組_1130521（0219後適用）.pdf)
- **International comparators:** Université Paris Cité, PSL and the University of Würzburg have single-signature or affiliation policies. Their stated aims are institutional visibility and accurate bibliometrics in databases and rankings. — [Université Paris Cité](https://u-paris.fr/language/en/single-signature-for-publications/); [PSL](https://psl.eu/en/signature-policy-on-scientific-publications); [Würzburg](https://www.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/37000400/Website_Forschung/Affiliation_Policy.pdf)

**Author identity: ORCID**
- ORCID Taiwan operates as a consortium, with the NYCU library as lead institution. Members aim for at least 70% of each university's researchers to link an ORCID iD to institutional systems. — [ORCID: Taiwan consortium](https://info.orcid.org/orcid-taiwan-revitalizes-connections-to-grow-institutional-adoptions)
- ORCID iDs must be linked by the record holder through authenticated OAuth; manual entry or collection of iDs is not allowed. Institutions can embed ORCID in thesis (ETD) workflows, recording the student's iD and the advisor's and committee's iDs in thesis metadata. — [ORCID zh-TW integration resources](https://info.orcid.org/zh-TW/%E5%A4%96%E5%B1%95%E8%B3%87%E6%BA%90/); [ORCID zh-TW ETD workflow](https://info.orcid.org/zh-TW/%E6%96%87%E6%AA%94/%E5%B7%A5%E4%BD%9C%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B/%E8%AB%96%E6%96%87%E5%92%8C%E8%AB%96%E6%96%87/)

**Visibility: open access and deposit**
- In Piwowar et al. (2018, *PeerJ*), OA articles received about 18% more citations than average, driven mainly by green (repository) and hybrid OA. Later authors note that this does not establish causation. — [PeerJ 4375](https://peerj.com/articles/4375/tweets); [arXiv 2103.14522](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.14522)

**What the rankings reward**
- THE weights research **quality** (30%) far above volume (papers per faculty, 5.5%) to remove incentives for low-value output. — [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/739285)
- QS removes self-citations, and THE will cap institutional self-citations from 2028. Growth from internal citation is discounted, while citations from outside the institution count fully. — [QS](https://support.qs.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019107580-Citations-per-Faculty); [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/744136)

### Inferences
**Concrete legitimate checklist for the student** (synthesis):
- (1) With the advisor, turn the thesis into a Scopus or WoS-indexed journal article or conference paper. This is the only route by which a master's project enters THE or QS bibliometrics.
- (2) Use the university's official English name and department exactly as the research office or library prescribes: no abbreviations alone, and include city and country "Taiwan."
- (3) Register an ORCID iD, link it to the university system, and after publication check that the Scopus author profile is correct; fix it through the Author Feedback Wizard if not. Report institution-level name problems to the library or research office, which holds IPW rights.
- (4) Deposit the thesis and accepted manuscripts open access (national thesis system, institutional repository, or green OA where the publisher allows), and share code and data so others can build on and cite the work.
- (5) Cite lab and department work wherever it is genuinely relevant. That is good practice and required, but not a ranking lever.

These actions are fully aligned with the ethics rules above, whereas citation padding is both ineffective and risky.

- The user's specific idea, citing professors' **master's theses** in their own master's thesis, scores poorly on both dimensions. It almost certainly does not touch Scopus-based ranking citations (other researchers are verifying thesis coverage). If any of the citations are irrelevant, it falls within COPE's and the MOE e-paper's definition of citation manipulation.
